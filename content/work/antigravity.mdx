<ContentSection id="product-ownership">

I owned Antigravity’s product delivery: the architecture, core systems and the team building the game. We shipped it on PulseChain and Base. I managed the engineers and kept the work on schedule while writing the core contracts, building the indexing paths, designing the keepers and modelling the economy myself.

The game unfolds in chapters. Players register, mine tokens, enter journeys and participate in lotteries. The rules change as the game moves into its next phase.

The challenge was making all of that behave as one product. A contract could be correct on its own and still leave a player looking at the wrong state, or waiting for an action that another part of the system needed to trigger.

</ContentSection>

<ContentSection id="game-state" title="Give the game one answer to “what happens next?”">

For the journey system, I put the current phase behind a shared phase manager. The other contracts consult it to determine which actions are available and when the game should move forward.

The product also needed a usable view of that state. I built the indexing layer to turn contract events into player and game data, and designed off-chain keepers—the processes responsible for triggering scheduled actions—to advance the game.

These relationships mattered to the engineers building the interfaces. They had a common model to work against: what the contracts allowed, what the player had done and what was due to happen next.

<ContentSection id="infrastructure" title="Make the missing infrastructure part of the plan">

At the time of the build, PulseChain lacked the hosted Graph support and native Chainlink randomness service the game needed. Those gaps became part of my system design.

I configured and self-hosted a Graph node for PulseChain and built a separate indexing path with Ponder. For the lotteries, I deployed the Chainlink randomness consumer on Base and designed the request-and-announcement lifecycle around that cross-chain boundary.

That flow included a guard against overlapping requests and a validity window for the response. Getting a random number was one step; the game also needed to know which request it belonged to and when it could be used.

</ContentSection>

<ContentSection id="economy" title="Work through the economy before committing its parameters">

Mining rewards, treasury payments and jackpots affect each other over the course of a journey. I built a Python simulation of those flows so we could examine the consequences of the parameters before putting them into the contracts.

The game also had to accommodate changes between journeys. I separated the storage layouts from the upgradeable contract logic, making the state that needed to survive an upgrade explicit. Unit, integration and fuzz tests, alongside audit remediation, supported that work.

</ContentSection>

<ContentSection id="delivery" title="Following the decisions through to delivery">

Antigravity reached mainnet on PulseChain and Base. My responsibility continued across the boundaries between contracts, infrastructure and the team building the surrounding product, through to timely delivery.

</ContentSection>
