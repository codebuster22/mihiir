Titus is my own prediction-market trading framework. I built it around the work that sits beneath a strategy: receiving market data, placing orders, managing on-chain positions and giving an operator a clear way to control the system.

A trading idea can look simple until it encounters partial fills, mismatched units, stale nonces or an order that is still active while the underlying tokens are being merged. I owned the core architecture and implementation, including the strategy SDK, execution service, deployment tooling and operating controls.

## Separate strategy decisions from execution authority

I organised the system into a market-data publisher, strategy applications and an executor, connected through NATS messaging. Strategies consume data and request actions through the SDK. They do not hold signing credentials; the executor handles authenticated trading and on-chain operations.

This boundary lets strategy code change without bringing wallet handling into every application. The SDK gives strategies a common way to read the order book and manage orders, while the execution service owns the exchange and settlement details.

## Treat money arithmetic and order state as engineering problems

I introduced explicit integer types for prices, share sizes and USDC amounts. These types distinguish quantities that might otherwise look like interchangeable numbers, and make rounding a deliberate operation.

Execution also needed to account for the difference between a reported fill and tokens settled on-chain. I used settled balances to size sells and suppressed sell orders before a merge to avoid conflicting operations on the same inventory.

One signing failure came from a local Safe nonce falling behind the chain. I changed the signing path to read the on-chain nonce immediately before signing, and limited the retry path to a confirmed reverted transaction. That addressed a concrete failure mode rather than retrying every uncertain submission.

## Make the system usable by an operator

I built a command-line interface for status, logs, restarts and rollback, alongside monitoring and Telegram alerts. A restart validates the configuration, saves a snapshot, checks health afterward and rolls back if the health check fails. Emergency-stop controls provide a separate way to halt strategies.

For a distinct client engagement, I took responsibility for strategy design as well as implementation and packaged a deployable distribution with onboarding and troubleshooting documentation. The underlying framework remained my own product. I also co-built the separate rewards-signals engine with another Chain Labs engineer.

## Delivery and current scope

The framework supported live trading strategies and a client deployment. I later retired the multi-region AWS deployment, preserving its infrastructure definitions and deployment artifacts for rebuilding. That deployment is no longer running.

Titus Intelligence, the related indexing and data product, is separate work. This case focuses on the trading framework and the decisions needed to make its execution and operation dependable.
