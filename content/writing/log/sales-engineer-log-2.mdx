Quite a day.

Faced a few demons today, and turns out… they weren’t actually that scary.

For the first time, I did outreach first thing in the morning.

There was still some procrastination:

“I’ll brew coffee, then start.”  
“Oh, let me clean my table first.”  
“One more small thing…”  
But I got through it.  
The goal was to finish outreach early so I could spend the rest of the day working on the Rust package and Uniswap v4 hooks.

Sent 4 cold outreach emails today — manually.

No AI-generated outreach. I only used ChatGPT to polish my drafts and learn along the way.

For now, my goal is to stay at 3–5 manual outreaches a day so I can collect data on where the friction and fear actually come from.

Main takeaways:

99% of the fear was about how the other person would perceive me.

Then I realized: they probably don’t have enough time to curse me, let alone hunt me down.

That made hitting send significantly easier.

I spent a lot of time staring at a blank email body.

Not because I couldn’t write, but because I didn’t know how to personalize the message in a way that felt genuinely relevant.

I have two assumptions about why this is happening. Need to validate them next.

I need to time-box each outreach from start to finish.

If I cross the limit, move on.

I’m trying to figure out how to apply the Optimal Stopping problem here.

Really interesting concept, btw. Chapter 1 of Algorithms to Live By explains it beautifully.

New hypothesis:

Either my offer isn’t defined clearly enough, or I’m reaching out to the wrong ICP.

Tomorrow, I’m blocking out some time specifically to understand the ICP better.

Question for people who’ve done a lot of outbound:

If you had to do cold outreach completely without AI, how much time would you typically spend researching/enriching each lead before writing the email?
